What it does

The Mac–iPhone handoff, for an Android phone.

Apple devices have always passed things between each other. An Android phone next to a Mac never could. FuseOS adds that link.

One clipboard

Copy text or an image on either device and paste it on the other. Everything you copy stays in a history on both, so you can find it again after a restart.

Text and images · both directions

Files, both ways

Drop a file on the Mac window, or pick one on your phone. It lands in Downloads on the other side. You can watch its progress and cancel it partway.

SHA-256 checked on arrival · up to 1 GB

From the share sheet

Choose FuseOS in any app's Share menu to send a link, a photo or some text to your other device. The FuseOS app doesn't need to be open.

Android today · Mac next

Links itself

Sign in to the same account on both devices and they find each other. There are no codes to scan and no pairing screen.

One account · automatic

How it moves

What you copy never touches our server.

The server only signs you in and helps your devices find each other. Your clipboard and your files go straight from one device to the other, encrypted, over your own network.

Your data

Every clip and file travels over a direct, encrypted connection between your two devices. It is never uploaded, never stored and never logged.

The server

It knows your account, which devices are yours and which are online, and tells each device where to find the other.

macOS

Preview

FuseOS.dmg · macOS 13 Ventura or later · Apple silicon

Download for Mac
  1. Open the DMG and drag FuseOS into Applications.
  2. Open FuseOS once. macOS will block it, because it isn't notarized by Apple yet.
  3. Go to System Settings → Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway. You only have to do this once.
  4. When it asks to find devices on your local network, click Allow. That permission is what lets your phone reach this Mac.

Android

Preview

FuseOS-preview.apk · Android 8.0 or later

Download for Android
  1. Download the APK on your phone and tap it to open it.
  2. If Android asks, let your browser install unknown apps. FuseOS isn't on the Play Store yet.
  3. Sign in, then allow notifications. They show when a file arrives, and they keep FuseOS connected in the background.
  4. Optionally, add the Send clipboard tile to Quick Settings. Then sending your clipboard to the Mac is one pull and one tap.

This is an early preview. Both devices need to be on the same Wi-Fi. Syncing between different networks isn't supported yet. Something broken? Open an issue.

Questions

Good to know.

Does my clipboard go to the cloud?

No. Clips and files go straight from one device to the other over your Wi-Fi, encrypted with AES-GCM using keys that belong to your devices. The server never receives them, so there is nothing of yours for it to store.

Why do I have to tap to send from Android?

Since Android 10, only the app on screen can read the clipboard. FuseOS keeps sending down to a single tap: the Quick Settings tile, the notification button, or the share sheet. Copying on the Mac needs no tap at all.

Why does macOS warn me when I open it?

Apple only removes that warning for apps signed through its paid developer program. The preview isn't enrolled yet, so you confirm it once in Privacy & Security and it won't ask again.

Is it open source?

Yes. The Android app, the Mac app and the server are all on GitHub.
